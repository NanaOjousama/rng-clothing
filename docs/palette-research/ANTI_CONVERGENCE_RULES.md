# Palette Anti-Convergence Rules

## Hard rules

1. **Topology before hue.** Select garment construction and color-block topology before palette story.
2. **No universal center formula.** A batch may not repeatedly use dark body + light center + tiny accent. Inner shirts must be broad garment regions; zippers/plackets remain construction lines.
3. **Rarity is color-neutral.** No hue family, metal, saturation level or dark/light balance maps to rarity.
4. **Gender is color-neutral.** Masculine/unisex designs may use light, pastel, bright, floral-adjacent or colorful stories when construction supports them.
5. **Recolors are variants.** Recoloring identical structural art never counts as a new garment design.
6. **Accent localization.** The highest-chroma role receives one focal region and at most one small echo.
7. **Quiet roles may be omitted.** Do not activate every color because the story provides it.
8. **Construction controls direction.** Blocks wrap, compress, terminate or disappear based on garment topology.

## Batch-balance minimums

For a future batch of twelve or more colored garments, require representation of:

- at least three light-primary garments;
- at least three middle-primary garments;
- at least two dark-primary garments;
- at least three warm-dominant stories;
- at least three cool-dominant stories;
- at least two neutral/low-saturation stories;
- at least one pastel/colorful masculine or unisex garment;
- at least one bright-sport or high-contrast story;
- no single palette story on more than two accepted candidates unless explicitly testing a coordinated collection.

These are future validation targets, not an instruction to generate a batch now.

## Topology-frequency caps

Track color topology separately from palette name. In one diverse batch, cap each dominant topology:

- broad inner/outer frame;
- shoulder/sleeve block;
- yoke/lower-body split;
- collar/cuff/hem triad;
- diagonal/asymmetric split;
- all-over pattern;
- light body/dark frame;
- single fabric plus localized accent.

No topology should dominate more than one third of a general-purpose batch. “Different palette story” does not waive the cap.

## Pairwise palette comparison

Compare each pair on:

- dominant value band;
- dominant temperature;
- saturation distribution;
- primary/secondary area ratio;
- structural-dark character;
- accent hue and location;
- color-block topology;
- directional continuation.

Reject palette-level near-duplicates when six or more axes overlap, especially if garment structures are also related.

## Directional rules

- Yokes and shoulder blocks continue across sides and back.
- Sleeve bands continue along the sleeve relationship.
- Ribbed cuffs and hems recur at every visible termination.
- Front-only shirts, ties, plackets and motifs compress or disappear at side/back.
- Back views receive coherent back fields, rear yokes, collar backs, hoods or seams—not mirrored front openings.
- Asymmetric closures preserve distinct left/right color mass.

## Failure signatures

- all masculine garments are navy/brown/olive/black;
- all premium garments are black, gold, purple, ivory or neon;
- every light garment lacks a strong structural dark;
- every dark garment is a near-black slab with one bright stripe;
- every sport story uses the same sleeve stripe and zipper layout;
- neon appears as scattered pixels rather than one construction-aligned region;
- material identity disappears because every ramp has the same spacing;
- a palette reads well enlarged but merges into one value at Farmer scale.

## Review gate

Palette approval requires a worn Farmer composite in Front | Right | Back | Left. Test both full color and a value-only view. If construction merges in value-only review, hue contrast is masking a structural or ramp problem. If the palette only differentiates two otherwise identical garments, it does not satisfy design diversity.
