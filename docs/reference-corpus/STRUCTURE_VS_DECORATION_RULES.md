# Structure vs Decoration Rules

## Classification

**Structural identity** determines the article before color or ornament:

- torso silhouette and volume;
- shoulder character;
- neckline and collar mass;
- opening/closure topology;
- outer/inner layer relationship;
- sleeve length, volume and body transition;
- waist and hem topology;
- pocket topology when pockets alter shell construction;
- side reconstruction and rear carrier/yoke/hood/seam.

**Decorative identity** enriches established construction:

- piping and edge trim;
- motifs, graphics, embroidery-like clusters and original insignia;
- button/zipper color after closure exists;
- small tabs and buckles;
- fabric pattern and texture;
- accent colors, highlights and shading.

Some features can cross the boundary. A broad sailor collar stripe is decorative, but the broad collar mass is structural. Large work-jacket patch pockets help establish shell weight and are structural; a tiny chest patch is decorative. A broad tabard panel is structural; the emblem on it is decorative.

## Generation order

1. Select a topology-distinct grammar.
2. Establish worn silhouette and body coverage.
3. Establish neck/collar, opening, layers, sleeves and hem.
4. Reconstruct side and back.
5. Validate article read with flat semantic colors.
6. Add major color blocking that follows construction.
7. Add at most one focal decorative system and compatible support treatment.
8. Shade and finish.

Decoration may not advance a structurally failed candidate.

## Structural uniqueness gate

Two candidates are structural duplicates when they substantially share torso mass, shoulder, collar/neckline, opening, layering, hem, sleeve relationship and rear carrier—even if their motifs and palettes differ. At least three high-impact axes should differ between adjacent batch members, and one must be silhouette/volume or opening/layer topology.

Names do not count. A semantic label such as “adventure jacket” cannot rescue the same fitted shell. Nor do rarity tags, color changes, extra buttons, one emblem, or additional shading.

## Decoration budgets

- Use one primary focal region: neck/upper chest, broad body field, diagonal wrap, hem/waist, or back—not all of them.
- Use one or two supporting systems that terminate or wrap logically.
- Keep quiet space around high-contrast motifs.
- Repeating patterns should follow fabric fields and simplify at sides/back.
- Avoid random isolated pixels. Every small cluster should read as a seam, fastener, fold, pocket, trim, or motif.

## Mental-removal tests

1. **Palette removal:** Replace decorative colors with two flat semantic fabrics. Is the article still identifiable?
2. **Motif removal:** Remove graphics, badges and trim. Do collar/opening/layering/hem remain interesting?
3. **Shading removal:** Flatten material ramps. Does the construction remain coherent?
4. **Name removal:** Can a worn Farmer viewer distinguish cardigan, open shirt, blazer, vest, field jacket and hoodie?

Failure at any structural test means revise grammar, not decoration.
