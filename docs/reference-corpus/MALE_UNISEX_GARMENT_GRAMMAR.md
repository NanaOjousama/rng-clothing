# Male and Unisex Garment Grammar

## Evidence policy

Primary masculine findings come from non-mixed male sources: Gentlemen's Collection, Jjoripong's Closet, male winter suits, Luny's shirts, masculine tracksuit outerwear, court-style menswear, and selected Grian pieces. Mixed/feminine sources contribute pixel technique, closures, color blocking, and genuinely unisex constructions only. Feminine shoulder exposure, bodice, corset, crop, and dress grammar is not silently relabeled masculine.

## Masculinity is construction, not a color preset

The corpus supports light, pastel, saturated, academic, sporty, refined, rugged, cute, fantasy, and historical masculine clothing. Masculine/unisex readability frequently comes from proportion and layer organization:

- regular or boxy torso mass rather than mandatory broad shoulders;
- clean collar/placket relationships;
- practical or formal pocket rhythm;
- undershirt/outer-layer separation;
- cuffs and hems that explain material and fit;
- measured looseness in sweatwear and workwear;
- tailoring in waistcoats, blazers, and period layers;
- clear sport bands and shoulder-to-sleeve continuation;
- restrained motifs placed into a structurally meaningful field.

Dark navy, brown, olive, and black are common in some sources, but colorful sweaters, pale shirts, bright sport shells, patterned knitwear, and formal contrast all remain masculine when construction is coherent.

| Family | Structural read | Suitable variation |
|---|---|---|

| Knit/sweatwear | material-coherent neck/cuff/hem | argyle, fair-isle bands, bright or soft palettes |

## Rules for appealing male/unisex variety

1. Select fit and opening before palette. A mint cardigan and rust cardigan are one family; a mint cardigan and rust overshirt are different garments.
2. Use looseness deliberately. Boxy tees, padded winter shells, soft cardigans, tailored waistcoats, and cropped bombers distribute volume differently.
3. Make layering wide enough to read. A visible tee or formal shirt must occupy a region, not a one-pixel center stripe.
4. Give side and back views their own carrier features: collar depth, cuff, pocket profile, yoke, hood, ribbed band, belt wrap, or tailored seam.
5. Use light/colorful palettes without apologizing for them. Construction maintains the masculine/unisex read.
6. Keep native animated Farmer arms visible for sleeveless layers. Vest art should explain armholes and inner sleeves, not erase the body.
7. Treat uniform, fantasy, and period references as topology studies. Original motifs and culture/copyright-safe abstraction are mandatory.

## Current project gaps for masculine clothing

- Open casual shirts and true overshirts are not distinct enough from jackets.
- Polo and henley partial-placket logic is not a first-class construction.
- Cardigan softness and blazer rigidity tend to collapse into the same open-front frame.
- Bomber, varsity, track, work, field, moto, and cropped fashion jackets need separate shell/hem/closure grammars.
- Rear yokes, knit backs, formal rear collars, and tabard/tunic backs are under-modeled.
- Male color-story selection overweights muted military families instead of casual, coastal, bright sport, pastel academic, and formal-light options.
