# Color Composition Findings

## Scope

This study records color *topology*, not a palette library. No source palettes were sampled for reuse. The dedicated project palette corpus remains separate.

## Effective topologies

1. **Single fabric with structural contrast:** one dominant body fabric; collar/cuff/hem use a neighboring value. Effective for tees, knits and premium minimal garments.
2. **Broad inner/outer layering:** two outer leaves frame a substantial inner shirt. Effective for cardigans, open shirts and blazers. The inner region must not collapse into a narrow bright center.
3. **Shoulder/sleeve block:** body and sleeves differ, often connected by a shoulder band. Effective for varsity, track, streetwear and padded shells.
4. **Upper yoke/lower body:** a broad shoulder or chest yoke contrasts with the lower torso. Effective for workwear, fieldwear and western/work shirts.
5. **Collar/cuff/hem triad:** small repeated contrast at all terminations establishes material and cohesion. Effective for knits, sweatwear and bombers.
6. **Asymmetric/diagonal mass:** two large fabric regions follow a wrap or moto closure. It must alter construction, not draw a stripe.
7. **All-over restrained pattern:** plaid, knit repeats or stripes occupy a fabric field while collar/opening remain quiet and clear.
8. **Light body with dark frame:** pale primary fabric bounded by dark collar/lapel/cuff/pocket structure. The corpus shows masculine/unisex clarity does not require a dark torso.
9. **Dark shell with broad light inner layer:** useful formal/open outerwear composition when the inner layer is a garment-shaped region rather than a slit.
10. **Localized accent:** a small high-saturation color is concentrated at one focal area and echoed once, not scattered.

## Preventing palette convergence

- Choose topology before specific hues.
- Cap repeated use of “dark body + light center + tiny accent” across a batch.
- Vary dominant value: light-primary, mid-primary and dark-primary garments should coexist.
- Vary temperature relationships: analogous warm, analogous cool, warm/cool split, neutral with saturated accent, and soft low-contrast stories.
- Let palette follow material: knit can use soft close values; sport shells tolerate crisp contrast; formalwear benefits from controlled value hierarchy; workwear needs durable midtone fields.
- Treat recolors as one grammar during diversity accounting.

## Directional color behavior

Large structural blocks wrap when the construction wraps: yokes, sleeve bands, ribbed hems, all-over patterns and sashes. Front-only inner layers, plackets and motifs compress or disappear. Back views need their own dominant field and may carry a quieter yoke or motif. Mirroring the front color map to the back produces implausible openings and repeated accents.

## Low-value color variation

Changing olive to navy, swapping trim color, moving one accent pixel, reversing highlight/shadow hue, or recoloring a repeated center stripe does not create a new design. These are variants inside a grammar and should not satisfy batch diversity.
