# Sailor Technical Reference Study

Scope: construction-only study of every image in `templates/fashionsense/sailor.zip`. Logos, branding, decorative artwork, photographic texture, and exact colors are excluded. This document derives physical architecture before any logical-sprite coordinates are chosen.

## Reference inventory

### `Screenshot_20260902_070445.png` — useful, primary technical flat

This is the strongest reference because it supplies matched front and back flats in two sleeve treatments.

- **Front collar shape:** broad paired sailor leaves begin near the outer shoulder-neck junction and sweep inward into an open V. The collar is a layer over the body, not a neckline stripe.
- **Back collar/flap shape:** broad shallow quadrilateral spanning most of the upper shoulder width. The top edge follows the rear neckline/shoulder line; the sides descend almost vertically before a clean horizontal lower termination.
- **Neck opening:** compact rounded rear neck feeding a moderate front V.
- **Collar width:** wide enough to dominate the entire upper chest and clearly exceed a polo collar.
- **Collar depth:** moderate in front; the rear is deliberately shallower than the front V.
- **Shoulder relationship:** collar begins high and wide, directly over the shoulder line.
- **Rear flap width:** most of the upper back between sleeve attachments.
- **Rear flap height:** upper-back only, roughly a minority of torso height.
- **Lower flap termination:** straight, controlled horizontal edge with lightly rounded/soft corners.
- **Side transition:** implied as a short shoulder wrap between the wide front collar and rear flap; it does not descend down the side torso.
- **Trim/border placement:** one or two narrow lines inset from, and parallel to, the complete collar/flap perimeter.
- **Tie/insert relationship:** narrow tie descends from the V apex and is subordinate to the collar mass.
- **Sleeve/body relationship:** sleeve seam begins outside the collar footprint; both long gathered sleeves and plain long sleeves preserve the same collar architecture.
- **Meaningful variation:** sleeve volume changes, but front/rear collar geometry remains stable.

## Derived physical architecture

The references support one canonical Sailor construction with controlled variants:

1. A woven body shell remains the root mass.
2. A broad front collar is a surface layer attached high across the neck/shoulder line. Its two leaves frame an open V and are wider than polo or crew-neck grammar.
3. The collar wraps across each upper shoulder through a short, compact side-depth mass.
4. A broad, shallow rear flap attaches immediately below the rear neckline. It spans most of the shoulder field, remains upper-back construction, and terminates in a clean straight or gently tapered lower edge.
5. Supported rear shapes are square, tapered-square, and shallow trapezoid. A tiny central rectangle and a deep hanging panel are unsupported.
6. Optional trim follows the component perimeter at a consistent inset. At Farmer scale this should degrade to a sparse one-logical-cell border, not a cage.
7. A front tie/insert is optional and subordinate. It attaches at the V apex and must not replace the collar mass.
8. Sleeve length and volume are independent of Sailor identity; the native sleeve contract remains unchanged.

## Directional reconstruction requirements

- **Front:** show the two broad collar leaves, their V relationship, and optional subordinate insert/tie. Border follows collar edges.
- **Right/left:** show a compact collar-depth cluster at the upper shoulder plus a short rearward handoff. No floating patch, long stripe, or diagonal slash.
- **Back:** show neck attachment first, then a shoulder-spanning shallow flap, perimeter separation, and a controlled lower edge. The body field must remain visible below it.
- **Continuity:** front side tips, side depth, and rear upper corners describe one wrapped component even though each direction is independently reconstructed.

## Evidence limits

- None of the references supplies a true orthographic side view, so exact side depth must be inferred conservatively from front/back attachment.
- The simplified `a.jpeg` rear is not evidence for omitting the canonical flap.
- The button-shirt flat supplies no Sailor-specific geometry.
- Exact stripe count, color, fabric texture, tie shape, sleeve shape, and dress proportions are not portable to this experiment.
