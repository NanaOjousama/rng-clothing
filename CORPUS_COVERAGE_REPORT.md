# Corpus Coverage Report

## Outcome

The local corpus is broad enough to rebuild an upper-garment design engine, but it is not balanced. It is strongest in jackets, formal/period clothing, fantasy/adventure layers, hoodies, sailor construction, and motif/surface variety. It is weakest in henleys, polos, moto jackets, modern overshirts, genuinely relaxed tees, non-hood streetwear, and non-military contemporary outerwear.

## Imbalance findings

- Jacket/outerwear and military/fieldwear evidence is overrepresented. Future concept selection must cap these rather than treating frequency as preference.
- Formal/period and fantasy/adventure have good high-salience construction evidence.
- Basics exist in quantity but not in equal structural depth; many are palette or motif variations.
- Feminine/alt references add valuable shoulder, neckline, crop, asymmetry, and volume evidence, but must retain their actual grammar labels.
- Lower-body and long-geometry references remain archived R&D and do not prove upper-body runtime support.

## Strong reference coverage

Crewneck/knit, cardigan, waistcoat, utility vest, cropped structured jacket, field/work jacket, blazer, period coat-top, sailor top, true hoodie, tunic, tabard, and armor-overlayer.

## Lacking references

Henley, polo-like tops, contemporary overshirts, moto jackets, lightweight unstructured blazers, modern non-hood streetwear, sportswear, and cyber garments whose identity comes from construction rather than graphics.

## Recommendation before generation resumes

Do not add more field jackets. First obtain or curate license-readable examples for the weak groups above, then visually code at least three examples per weak group. Generation should resume only after the machine-readable grammar validator, Farmer composite renderer, pairwise structural gate, and label-blind human review form are implemented.
