## A. Construction language

Strongly confirmed:

- compact, waist-terminated outer jackets;
- tall folded or high military collars and clear lapel recesses;
- paired left/right outer-shell organization around a fitted underlayer;
- upper-chest panels and paired flap-like utility construction;
- fitted shirts and waistcoat-over-shirt layering;
- yoke/shoulder organization and disciplined center closures;
- structured cuffs and abrupt waist/hem termination.

Refinement: the references do not support making every garment a split-panel jacket. Fitted white shirts, dark waistcoats, high-neck underlayers, rust civilian tailoring, and long black formal outerwear recur enough to justify separate branches.

## B. Surface-design language

The recognizable surface vocabulary is geometric and construction-bound:

- short paired upper-chest rectangles/flaps;
- shoulder tabs connected to collar/yoke construction;
- lapel/collar edge lines;
- one or two vertical front seams;
- a short horizontal chest division;
- dark waist termination;
- sparse cuff lines;
- one controlled chest/waist diagonal or paired angular rhythm.

The full harness networks visible in combat outfits are not shirt decoration. V3 reduces them to one support gesture. Dense crossed straps would become noise and incorrectly make equipment the garment's primary identity. A modest original shoulder/chest crest can instead serve as a premium focal feature.

## C. Color language

Confirmed dominant relationships:

- warm tan/brown outer cloth over olive, charcoal, cream, or muted red fitted inner layers;
- field olive paired with beige and black-brown;
- white/cream shirts under olive, brown, or charcoal vest forms;
- formal charcoal/black with muted cream and restrained burgundy/brown;
- rust civilian outerwear over black or deep neutral layers;
- dark leather-like values at belts, cuffs, seams, and waist termination.

The profile uses five newly authored, non-sampled palette stories: garrison tan, field olive, formal charcoal, civilian rust, and high-contrast field.

## D. Silhouette language

- cropped/compact and waist-emphasized for the military core;
- fitted-to-regular torso mass rather than oversized volume;
- strong upper collar mass tapering into a compact waist;
- uninterrupted fitted underlayers beneath more segmented outer garments;
- long/full dark outerwear only in the civilian/formal secondary branch.

The 16×16 shirt backend cannot render a genuinely knee-length coat. Dark-formal influence must therefore appear through closure, value, collar, and fuller torso termination without claiming true coat length.

## E. Decorative and utility language

Confirmed utility details include paired chest flaps, shoulder tabs, pocket seams, sparse buttons, cuffs, belt-like waist divisions, restrained chest straps, and regiment-style patch placement. Copied insignia and exact buckle maps are excluded; original split-wing, shield-like, or angular badge geometry is permitted within rarity ceilings.

AoT recognition should come from the compound relationship among cropped shell, high collar/lapel, upper panels, contrasting underlayer, dark waist rhythm, and—on premium pieces—a modest emblem focal point. An emblem supports construction; it does not substitute for it.

## F. Civilian sub-branch

The civilian branch uses fitted dark tops, rust or burgundy outer layers, pale tailored shirts, waistcoats, gray/black casual layering, and formal black outerwear. It retains disciplined collars and waist structure but reduces utility geometry. This branch is essential to avoiding six near-identical tan jackets.

## G. Military core branch

The core branch combines:

1. compact cropped tan/brown outer shell;
2. structured folded/high collar and lapel;
3. visible olive/dark/pale fitted inner layer;
4. shoulder and upper-chest organization;
5. paired pocket/flap suggestion;
6. dark leather-like seam or waist accent;
7. one simplified harness/utility rhythm;
8. modest original regiment-style badge or crest;
9. strong outline and controlled shading.

Rare 1 must preserve this combination. Brown palette alone will not satisfy recognition.

## H. Required Stardew-scale simplifications

- exact faction patches and winged insignia: never reproduce; translate premium recognition into an original compact split-wing/crest geometry;
- paired pockets: reduce to short paired geometry, not illustrated pockets;
- epaulettes: absorb into a shoulder/yoke block;
- harness network: reduce to one paired, diagonal, or waist gesture;
- buckles/buttons: one small tab rhythm at most;
- coat length: translate through fuller/darker torso and hem treatment;
- fabric texture and folds: keep the permanent FS-HD cluster grammar;
- full uniform ensemble: generate only the top; do not imply pants, boots, capes, or gear exist.

## V3 integration decision

The existing V3 systems remain sufficient: shoulder/yoke/side blocks, lapel/collar/cuff/hem/piping trim, military-repeat and chevron patterns, compact `star`/military geometry reinterpreted through profile aliases, construction-bound topology, and rarity hierarchy. No renderer category or coordinate template was added. The corrected adapter also imposes per-tier richness floors and complexity ceilings so Rare stays premium without consuming future Legendary/Mythic design space.
